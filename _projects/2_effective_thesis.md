This ongoing Effective Thesis fellowship project studies how closely theoretical LLM exposure
translates into observed task-level automation, and how that relationship evolves as frontier AI
capabilities improve. The central motivation is that a task an AI system *could* affect is not
necessarily a task that people actually delegate to AI.

The project links OpenAI and Eloundou et al.'s theoretical exposure measures to observed automation
in Anthropic's Economic Index using O\*NET Task IDs. The initial analysis focuses on the validated
April and May 2026 data, distinguishing Anthropic's automation share from its broader usage-share
measure. The next stage will audit historical Economic Index releases and construct a harmonized
task-by-time panel where the underlying measures are sufficiently comparable.

Planned outputs include:

- A reproducible task-level dataset connecting theoretical exposure with observed automation.
- Descriptive and fixed-effects analyses of where exposure and realized automation converge or
  diverge.
- A limited mechanism analysis using O\*NET and WORKBank measures of human agency, worker
  preferences, and expert-assessed feasibility.
- A working paper and replication package with documented robustness checks and limitations.

Any event-study or capability-shock extension will be treated as causal only if the historical data
support a credible design; otherwise, the contribution will remain explicitly descriptive and
explanatory.

**Why it matters for safety:** labor-market forecasts often treat technical exposure as if it were
realized automation. Measuring the gap between capability and actual delegation can clarify where
organizational constraints, worker preferences, oversight requirements, and human agency continue
to matter as frontier systems improve.
