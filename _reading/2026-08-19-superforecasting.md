Currently reading. Tetlock and Gardner examine why some forecasters consistently outperform
others and argue that good prediction is a trainable practice: decompose difficult questions, use
base rates, express uncertainty probabilistically, update as evidence changes, and keep score. The
connection to AI safety and economics is methodological. Claims about model capabilities, labor
displacement, and transformative-AI timelines are forecasts, even when they are presented as
confident narratives; calibration and disciplined updating make those claims more accountable.
